Prologue

The interface from the film, on a laptop

In Minority Report, detectives sort evidence by sweeping their hands through the air. The film needed gloves and a wall of glass. Today a laptop camera and an open hand-tracking model are enough to try the same idea on an ordinary desk.

Precog turns each camera frame into 21 points per hand, compares them with gestures I recorded, and acts on macOS directly.

Act I

Seeing

From camera pixels to a hand you can measure.

Hand tracking

Twenty-one points per hand

Every frame goes through Google's MediaPipe hand landmarker. It returns the wrist, four joints on the thumb and four on each finger, plus a guess at left or right.

Precog never looks at pixels after this step. Poses, swipes, pinches and the standby check are all geometry on these points.

Hover a point for its name. Amber: the hand-size ruler, wrist to middle knuckle. Circle: the palm centre.

Making hands comparable

Near or far, the same hand

A hand close to the camera looks twice as big as one at arm's length. So every hand is redrawn relative to its own wrist and divided by its own size: the distance from the wrist to the middle-finger knuckle.

Movement is measured the same way. The palm travels in hand-widths, not pixels, so a swipe is the same swipe whether you sit close or lean back.

Dynamic time warping

A slow swipe and a fast swipe are the same swipe

People never repeat a movement at the same speed. Precog resamples every movement to 16 steps, then lines them up with dynamic time warping: each step is paired with the step it resembles most, as long as the order is kept.

The cost of a pair is the difference in hand shape plus 0.35 × the difference in path. Averaged along the cheapest route through the grid, that gives one distance.

Teaching it

Six takes set the threshold

For each take, Precog measures how far it is from its nearest other take. A take far from the rest (a misread hand, a much slower attempt) is set aside. The threshold comes from the spread of what's left, with a floor and a ceiling.

Acting

Five modes, five colours

Precog always shows which mode it is in

The colour of the hand skeleton in the corner of the screen says what your hands will do right now. Most of the time you are in gesture mode; the others open for one task and close again.

Under the trackpad

Sliding desktops the way a trackpad does

The obvious approach, pressing Ctrl+→ in software, does nothing: macOS ignores synthetic key presses for switching desktops. A real three-finger swipe sends a private "dock swipe" event with a progress value instead.

Precog sends that same sequence: Began, a stream of Changed as your hand moves, then Ended to commit or Cancelled to spring back. Past a third of the way, or with a flick, it commits.

Clicking in mid-air

A pinch that behaves like a mouse button

The cursor follows your knuckles, which barely move when you pinch. As thumb and index close in, the cursor slows down; once they touch, it freezes, so the click lands where you aimed.

Press and release use different distances (0.32 and 0.48 hand sizes), so a trembling pinch can't click twice. It only becomes a drag after 45 px of movement.

Illustrative signal; the thresholds are Precog's real settings.

Off switch

Ten spread fingers put it to sleep

A gesture interface needs a way to say "not now". Both hands, all fingers out and spread wider than a normal open hand, held still for 0.8 s: Precog stops listening until you do it again.

Wider than normal is measured, not guessed. ./precog-run calibrate records your relaxed open hand; my own sits at a finger gap of 0.45 at the 99th percentile, so the switch needs 0.46.

Live

The recogniser, running in this page

This is Precog's matcher ported to JavaScript, loaded with my real recordings. Each bar is the best distance to one gesture; the black tick is its threshold. Cross it and the gesture fires.

Without a camera, it is watching a stream of my recordings. Turn the camera on and it watches you: swipe left or right to jump between sections, expand both hands for the contents, ten fingers for standby.

Nothing is recorded or sent.
