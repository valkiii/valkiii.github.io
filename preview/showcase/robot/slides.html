Act I · The brain · Choosing the game

Start with a game small enough to learn

Connect 4: 7 columns, 6 rows, one decision per turn. Which column?

Simple rules, but about 4.5 trillion legal positions. Far too many to memorise, so an AI has to learn to generalise.

Sparring partner 1 · Random

Drops a token in any legal column.

Sparring partner 2 · Rules

Win if you can. Otherwise block. Otherwise build a three. Otherwise play near the centre. Beats random in ~99% of games.

Act I · The brain · Reinforcement learning

Learning by playing

Nobody teaches it good moves. It plays, gets a reward when the game ends, and learns which moves lead to wins.

Under the hood · Q-learning Q(s,a) ← Q(s,a) + α·[ r + γ·max Q(s′,a′) − Q(s,a) ] Nudge the old guess toward "reward now + best value next". α learning rate · γ = 0.95 future weight · ε random-move rate, 1.0 → 0.05.

The AI (purple) must answer: how good is each column?

A Q-value is the reward the agent expects if it plays that column. Here green threatens four in a row, so blocking in D scores highest. Illustrative values.

Act I · The brain · Deep Q-Network

From a lookup table to a neural network

Tabular Q-learning

One score per board it has seen: 187,000 entries after training, and still clueless on a board it has never met.

Deep Q-Network (DQN)

Replace the table with a neural network. The board goes in, 7 scores come out. Similar boards get similar scores, so knowledge carries over.

Lesson: an agent only gets as good as its sparring partners. Trained against random play, both learned to beat random play.

Act I · The brain · Convolutional network

A network that sees lines

Diagonal filter · 4 in a row found

Four in a row is a visual pattern. A convolutional network slides small filters across the board, the way your eye scans for lines.

InputTwo 6 × 7 layers: my tokens, your tokens Patterns6 convolution layers learn threats, blocks and lines Two headsHow good is this board? How much better is each column? Size~0.5 M parameters, trained on a MacBook's M1 GPU
Under the hood · dueling DQN Q(s,a) = V(s) + A(s,a) − meana′ A(s,a′) V judges the whole board, A ranks the 7 columns. Learning gets steadier when most moves are equally fine.
Act I · The brain · What went wrong in training

Training fought back

01 · Exploding scores

Q-values grew without limit

Each estimate fed the next one until the numbers blew up. Fix: discount 0.95, clamp to ±100, clip the gradients.

02 · Forgetting

New opponent, old skills gone

Switching sparring partners erased what it knew (catastrophic forgetting). Fix: a curriculum with 4,000-game overlaps and a memory that keeps old games.

03 · Fake self-play

Playing a stale copy of itself

Its "self" was a snapshot from 500 games ago. Fix: a league of 5 past versions. Win 60% to move up a tier.

04 · Too little feedback

Win or lose is a weak signal

One reward per game says little about which move mattered. Fix: +2 for a block, −3 for missing a block, −5 for missing a win.

Act II · The eyes · Software to hardware

Give the brain a body

An SO-101: an open-source arm with 6 servo motors, driven from Python with Hugging Face's lerobot.

It has no eyes

So a USB camera goes on the wrist ("eye in hand"). It sees exactly where it is about to reach.

The board lies flat

Not the upright Connect 4 rack. A flat grid on the table is what checkers and chess look like too.

A real move

Go to the token supply, grab one, carry it, drop it in the chosen cell.

Act II · The eyes · Computer vision

How the robot reads the board

1 · FIND IT

Find the board

Keep bright pixels, take the biggest shape, reduce it to 4 corners.

2 · FLATTEN IT

Straighten it

A perspective warp makes a flat 700 × 600 image: 100 px per cell.

3 · FILTER COLOURS

Find the tokens

HSV colour filters keep green (me) and purple (the AI). The rest goes black.

4 · READ IT

Make it numbers

Each token's centre gives its cell: the 6 × 7 grid the AI reads.

Under the hood · HSV Hue, saturation, value: colour is kept apart from brightness, so a shadow changes V, not H. Green = hue 35–90, purple = 125–165.
Sanity check from physics Tokens can't float: one with an empty cell below it is glare, so it is removed. Colour filters still loved daylight and hated lamps.
Act II · The eyes · Where things are

From pixels to millimetres

The AI only needs "purple in D3". The arm needs to know where D3 is, in millimetres.

Homography

A flat surface seen at an angle is a stretched rectangle. Four known points undo the stretch.

ArUco markers

Printed squares the camera finds and identifies by itself. Their corners are the known points.

Lens fix

The wide-angle lens bends straight lines (~7 mm). A one-time lens calibration fixes it.

Under the hood [u v 1]ᵀ ∝ H · [x y 1]ᵀ H is a 3 × 3 matrix with 8 unknowns. Each point pair gives 2 equations, so 4 points solve it; 16 marker corners make it robust.
Act III · The arm · The first approach

First attempt: teach every cell

Move the arm by hand over each cell and save its joint angles. For a new spot, blend the nearest saved poses.

What was built

40 hand-taught poses. A new target borrowed a weighted blend of the 8 nearest.

Why it could never generalise

Move the board 5 mm and every pose is wrong. Averaging joint angles is not averaging positions, so blends drifted. A new game means teaching every square again: 64 for chess.

Act III · The arm · The key idea

Forward and inverse kinematics

Forward · angles → position

Given every joint angle, where is the fingertip? Chain the links: turn, move along, turn, move along. One answer, easy.

Inverse · position → angles

Given where the fingertip must go, which angles get it there? The reverse question: maybe several answers, maybe none.

Think of a cup. You don't plan "shoulder 30°, elbow 70°". You look at the cup and your arm works it out. IK gives the robot that skill.

Act III · The arm · How the solver works

Solving it: guess, check, adjust

1 · Guess

Start from 3 typical reaching shapes, pointed at the target.

2 · Check

Run forward kinematics: how far is the fingertip from the target?

3 · Adjust

Change the angles in the direction that shrinks that distance. Repeat until it's under 1 mm.

Gripper preference · stay vertical, lean out only if needed

Under the hood · kinematics.py min ‖FK(q) − target‖² + (30 mm/rad · tilt error)² Least squares within the joint limits. Position wins; tilt only has to be roughly right. Sketch shows a simulated 2-joint arm.

Act III · The arm · The detours

What we tripped over

A LiDAR on the wrist?

Not needed

±6 cm accuracy and a 20 cm blind zone. The table is flat, so height is already known.

Two unit systems

Degrees vs −100…100

Scripts spoke different units and loaded different calibration files: up to ~9° apart.

The printer

100 mm became 97.5

The mat printed slightly small. One scale factor fixed it.

Lazy servos

They stop ~1.5° short

Under load. Now it reads the encoders, nudges, and repeats until they match.

The far row

Taught at a tilt

The model never saw that pose, so it guessed 5 mm too low. Teach the pose you will use.

The lens

Fisheye bends lines

Predicted crosses drifted ~7 mm. A one-time lens calibration straightens the image.

Most of these looked like an arm problem. Most were measurement problems.
